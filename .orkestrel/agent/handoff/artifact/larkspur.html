Larkspur Context Lab

A small model, Qwen 3.5 or Gemma 4 at the 2B or 4B size, works one support shift: 10 requests in a row, each needing a fact from 48 earlier messages. Each strategy decides what the model reads per request. The per-topic records, the ledger’s records briefing, run against the full view, the control.

Where things stand

    Records against the full view, per setting

    Every figure counts audited passes out of 10 requests per reworded copy of the Larkspur shift; each copy rewords the 10 requests and keeps every fact. A range runs from the low end to the high end: an ambiguous audit verdict counts as a fail at the low end and as a pass at the high end. With d the per-copy difference, records minus the full view, a pair clears at an end when mean(d) − 2·sd(d)/√n > 0. The strict end pairs both low ends, the lenient end pairs both high ends, and across pairs the records low end with the full view high end.

    The records’ credit-check misses state every figure and leave out the “it fits” verdict. g06, Kenji’s shipping reply, passes in no arm and no setting; the following section shows why.

    g06, Kenji’s shipping reply

    A pass needs four things at once: a message to Kenji, the tracking number, no delivery date although the order lookup returns the carrier’s estimate, and no claim that the gift note was added although the lookup says its text is not recorded. Each cell counts audited correct replies of 4 copies (a range where an ambiguous verdict changes the count) and, under it, the replies that carry a delivery date. The live columns ran g06 alone; the replay columns change one thing in the recorded records calls, each base replay reproducing its record byte for byte.

    The rule reaches every prompt in both arms, and records and the full view fail alike, so g06 measures the model and the goal rather than the context strategy. The plain rule is “never put a delivery date, a carrier estimate included, in a customer reply”; the recorded rule says “never promise”.
