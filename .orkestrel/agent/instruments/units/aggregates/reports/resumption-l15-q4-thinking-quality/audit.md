## Grading conventions

Semantic pass means the reply answers the substantive request using the facts and instructions eligible through the goal's inclusive seed boundary. Corrections replace earlier values. Historical mentions used to reject a stale value do not fail. Equivalent dates and amounts pass. Optional omissions do not fail; unsupported material assertions, including completed notifications and promised credit timing, do.

The authored system controls the actual protocol. `recall` replaces `search_history`; the final reply replaces `send_reply`. A final reply is not evidence of a separately delivered message or changed order. Seeded tool calls and lookup text embedded in recall results are not fresh actions. The system lookup rule applies to requests concerning the named customers' known orders/accounts even when the immediate question does not repeat the identifier. A per-goal list containing only `send_reply` does not waive that rule.

The walk-in counter question is a generic desk-policy question with no order/account reference. It needs no fresh lookup. By contrast, a list of required calls or shipment chases concerns the linked orders/accounts. The narrow listed-tool verdict requires the authored list after protocol translation; it does not silently add the broader system lookup rule.

The packet does not expose the complete working view at every decision. Consequently, the conditional system obligation to recall a fact absent from view cannot independently be reconstructed for an otherwise compliant row. Explicit recall obligations in the gift-note and Sigrid callback goals are graded directly. Every lookup failure stated in the judgments follows from the goal-local actions, without assumptions about hidden context.

Complete work passes only with semantically satisfactory work and both applicable action scopes fulfilled. `served` is an operational status, not a quality verdict.
